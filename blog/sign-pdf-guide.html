Engineering Deep-Dive: How White-Paper Background Removal Works in Client RAM

When you upload an image of a signature written on paper, 95% of competitors slap the entire image onto the PDF. Because paper is never 100% white, this creates an unsightly grayish-white rectangle that hides printed document text underneath. LocalDoc resolves this directly in browser memory by reading the ImageData buffer (32-bit RGBA pixel array). For every pixel, the luminosity is calculated: L = 0.299*R + 0.587*G + 0.114*B. Pixels with $L > 205$ have their alpha channel set to $0$ (fully transparent), while transitional anti-aliased edge pixels ($145 < L \le 205$) are proportionally blended. The result is crisp, authentic signature ink that floats seamlessly over contract lines.

3. Moving Your Signature: How to Place It on the Exact Line or Box

Legal documents come in countless layouts. A government W-9 tax form features a narrow rectangular box labeled “Sign Here”; a real estate lease has a clean dotted line on page 14; a non-disclosure agreement requires initials on every page footer and a full execution block at the end.

LocalDoc provides a hardware-accelerated, free-floating draggable viewport with scale-invariant coordinate tracking:

  • Smooth Drag-and-Drop Repositioning: Simply click and hold your mouse on the signature card (or touch and slide on a smartphone or tablet screen) to move the signature anywhere across the document. The box moves fluidly at 60 frames per second without stutter or lag.
  • Scale-Invariant Relative Coordinates: Many online PDF tools suffer from annoying coordinate drift: if you zoom in to 150% to place a signature precisely on a narrow dotted line, the signature jumps to a completely different location when downloaded. LocalDoc stores signature positions as normalized fractional coordinates (relX, relY, relW, relH) from $0.0$ to $1.0$. No matter your screen zoom or device pixel ratio, the signature remains locked to the exact physical point on the document.
  • The PDF Y-Axis Cartesian Inversion: In web browsers, the coordinate origin $(0,0)$ sits at the top-left corner with Y increasing downward. In the PDF specification (ISO 32000-1), the origin $(0,0)$ sits at the bottom-left corner with Y increasing upward. When burning signatures, LocalDoc calculates:
    pdfX = relX * pageWidth
    pdfY = (1.0 - relY - relH) * pageHeight
    This mathematical inversion ensures 100% millimeter accuracy on desktop printers and PDF viewers.
  • Corner Scaling Controls (+ and −): Need the signature slightly smaller to fit inside a tight table cell, or larger to fill a wide corporate signature block? Click the direct + and − buttons attached to the box corners to scale the signature with zero distortion.
  • Multi-Page Document Navigation: Use the page navigation bar (← Prev Page and Next Page →) to inspect any page of multi-page agreements and place multiple initials or signatures wherever needed.

10. Legal Enforceability: ESIGN Act, eIDAS, and SES vs QES Explained

One of the most critical questions business owners, freelance contractors, and corporate legal teams ask is: “Is an electronic signature generated in a web browser legally enforceable in a court of law?”

To answer this with total legal accuracy, it is essential to understand the statutory distinction between Electronic Signatures (SES) and Digital Signatures with PKI Certificates (QES):

1. Simple Electronic Signature (SES) — LocalDoc Standard

An electronic symbol, mark, sound, or process attached to a document with demonstrable intent to sign.

Statutory Basis: US ESIGN Act (15 U.S.C. § 7001), UETA, and EU eIDAS (Regulation No 910/2014, Article 25(1)).
Enforceability: 100% legally binding for 99% of commercial transactions: NDAs, employment agreements, residential leases, vendor invoices, SaaS contracts, and freelance statements of work.

2. Qualified Electronic Signature (QES) — PKI Certificates

A cryptographic signature generated with a hardware token or smartcard issued by a government-accredited Trust Service Provider (TSP).

When Required: Formal court affidavits, last wills and testaments, codicils, family court adoptions, and government real estate land conveyances in specific regulated jurisdictions.
Industry Reality: No browser-based web application alone can generate a true QES without external PKI smartcards or certified identity verification.

Under the United States Federal ESIGN Act, Section 101(a) explicitly mandates that “a signature, contract, or other record relating to such transaction may not be denied legal effect, validity, or enforceability solely because it is in electronic form.” When you place your drawn, typed, or uploaded signature on a contract in LocalDoc and click Apply Signature, your intent to authenticate the document is legally demonstrated.

Similarly, in the European Union under eIDAS, Article 25(1) states that “an electronic signature shall not be denied legal effect and admissibility as evidence in legal proceedings solely on the grounds that it is in electronic form.” LocalDoc burns your signatures directly into the document's ISO 32000 content stream, ensuring visual persistence and document finality across all PDF rendering software worldwide.

Handling Password-Protected & Encrypted PDF Agreements

Some PDF agreements come with an Owner or Permissions Password that restricts editing, printing, or form modification. In strict adherence to PDF security standards, LocalDoc's client-side engine cannot inject signature vector streams into an encrypted file without authorization. If your file is protected:

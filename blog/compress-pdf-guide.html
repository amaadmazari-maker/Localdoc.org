1. Why PDF Documents Bloat to Enormous Sizes

Portable Document Format (PDF) files are container architectures combining typography glyphs, vector geometries, raster bitmaps, and metadata catalogs. When documents are exported from Microsoft Word, Adobe InDesign, or phone scanner apps, they frequently suffer from structural inefficiency:

The 4 Primary Sources of PDF Bloat

  1. Uncompressed Raster Bitmaps: Phone cameras take 12 to 48-megapixel photos at 300+ DPI. When saved as raw uncompressed JPEG or TIFF streams, a single scan page consumes 8 to 15 megabytes.
  2. Full Font Embedding: When a document uses a font like Helvetica or Times New Roman, standard PDF exporters frequently embed the entire 256-character Unicode font file rather than subsetting only the 30 glyphs actually used.
  3. Redundant XObject Dictionaries: Repeated letterheads, company logos, and background stamps are often cloned on every single page rather than referenced via a shared indirect object pointer.
  4. Legacy Uncompressed Cross-Reference (XRef) Tables: Older PDF 1.4 architectures store object lookup tables in ASCII plain text rather than modern compressed binary streams introduced in PDF 1.5.

Frequently Asked Questions: PDF Compression

Does PDF compression make text blurry? ▾

No. Vector typography, TrueType font outlines, and mathematical tables are compressed losslessly using FlateDecode. Characters remain perfectly crisp at 800% zoom.

Are my compressed PDF files uploaded to your servers? ▾

Never. LocalDoc runs its compression engine 100% inside your browser's private WebAssembly memory sandbox. You can verify this by opening DevTools Network tab—zero network calls are executed.

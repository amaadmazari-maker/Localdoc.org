1. The Compliance Mandate: Why Dual-Sided Photocopies Are Required

Whether applying for a commercial mortgage, opening a business bank account, leasing a vehicle, or completing immigration verification (KYC/AML), institutions almost universally demand a single-page photocopy showing both the Front and Back of your official identity document.

Regulatory compliance requires simultaneous inspection of two distinct security zones:

  • Front Side: Primary facial portrait, legal name, date of birth, issuing date, and micro-printed optical guilloche patterns.
  • Back Side: Machine Readable Zone (MRZ), 2D PDF417 barcodes, organ donor markers, residential addresses, and biometric chip antenna outlines.

Traditionally, compiling this required visiting a local print shop where clerks photocopied identity cards on shared machines, leaving unencrypted digital image copies cached on public hard drives. LocalDoc eliminates this critical exposure by synthesizing true physical-scale photocopies inside your private browser tab.

2. ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 Physical Sizing & A4 Layout Mathematics

A frequent issue with amateur scans is incorrect document scaling. When an applicant pastes two smartphone snapshots into Microsoft Word, cards are often stretched or shrunk, causing verification scanners to reject the upload.

Under international standard ISO/IEC 7810 (ID-1 Format), all standard credit cards, national identity cards, and driver's licenses adhere to strict dimensions:

Geometric Layout Specifications at 300 DPI

  • Physical Card Dimensions: Exact width $W = 85.60\text{ mm}$ (3.370 in); height $H = 53.98\text{ mm}$ (2.125 in); corner radius $R = 3.18\text{ mm}$.
  • Digital Pixel Matrix at 300 DPI: Width equals $\frac{85.60}{25.4} \times 300 = 1011\text{ px}$; height equals $\frac{53.98}{25.4} \times 300 = 638\text{ px}$.
  • A4 Canvas Allocation (2480 × 3508 px): The front face is centered horizontally ($X = 734\text{ px}$) in the upper hemisphere ($Y = 480\text{ px}$). The reverse face is placed directly beneath it ($Y = 1420\text{ px}$) with a standardized 302 px (25.5 mm) vertical gap for attestation notes.

4. Honest Technical Limitations: What Automated ID Card Scanners Cannot Do

Engineering transparency requires noting physical optical boundaries where client-side software reaches its threshold:

3 Real Limitations to Keep in Mind

  • Holographic Flash Reflections & Specular Blindness: Modern identity cards feature diffractive optical variable devices (DOVDs) and retro-reflective laminates. Firing a smartphone camera flash directly at a plastic card creates an opaque, mirror-like specular bloom that completely washes out underlying text and dates of birth. You must capture cards under indirect ambient daylight with camera flash disabled.
  • Chipped or Heavily Beveled Corners: If a physical card has chipped corners, peeling lamination, or severe edge abrasions from wallet friction, automatic quad edge snapping may calculate the boundary 1–2 mm inside the actual card edge. LocalDoc provides interactive corner adjustment handles to fine-tune edge placement before final rendering.
  • Consumer Printer "Fit to Page" Scaling Errors: While LocalDoc embeds mathematically exact 300 DPI metadata in the exported PDF, if an end-user prints the document through Adobe Acrobat or Apple Preview with "Fit to Page" or "Shrink to Fit" toggled, the printer driver will downscale the card by 4% to 7%, violating bank 1:1 physical size checks. Always instruct users to select "Actual Size (100%)" in print dialogs.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Why do banks require both sides of an ID card on one page?

Financial compliance regulations (KYC/AML) mandate that identity documents be archived as a single unalterable unit. Splitting front and back faces into separate image files introduces tampering vulnerabilities and doubles document indexing labor.

How does LocalDoc guarantee 1:1 physical print accuracy?

Our engine strictly maps the 85.60 × 53.98 mm ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 standard to an uncompressed 300 DPI vector PDF canvas. When printed without scaling, measuring the printed card with a physical ruler yields the exact millimeters of the real plastic card.

Is my personal identity data vulnerable to cloud scrapers?

No. LocalDoc contains zero server-side endpoints. All canvas compositing, image binarization, and PDF generation occur 100% inside your client browser RAM. Your national identity numbers never leave your device.

Can I scan other plastic cards like driver's licenses and membership badges?

Yes. Any card conforming to standard credit card dimensions (85.6 × 54 mm), including driver's licenses, employee badges, transit passes, and student IDs, works seamlessly with our ID card compiler.

US Visa Photo Requirements: Complete DS-160 & 2x2 Inch Specification Guide

AM Amaad Mazari Lead Systems Architect Updated October 2026 8 Min Read

Executive Summary & Rapid Checklist

The United States Department of State enforces strict mathematical tolerances on all photos submitted for nonimmigrant visas (DS-160), immigrant visas (DS-260), and the Diversity Visa (DV) Green Card Lottery. Photos failing biometric proportions, background lighting, or eyeglasses bans trigger automated rejections at the embassy portal.

  • Digital Dimensions: Exactly square, 600 x 600 pixels minimum up to 1200 x 1200 pixels maximum.
  • Physical Print Dimensions: 2 x 2 inches (51 x 51 mm) printed on photo-quality paper.
  • File Size Cap: Strictly 240 KB or less in JPEG format (sRGB color space).
  • Head Proportion: Head height must be between 50% and 69% of the total frame (1 to 1 3/8 inches).
  • Eyeglasses Ban: Eyeglasses are strictly forbidden. No exceptions for cosmetic or reading glasses.

1. The Two Formats: Digital Upload vs. Physical Interview Prints

Applying for a US visa involves two distinct photographic checkpoints. In the initial digital phase, applicants must upload an image file into the Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC) during the online submission of Form DS-160 or Form DS-260. The portal's automated computer vision system analyzes the file in real time for resolution, pixel aspect ratio, color profile, and compression density.

In the second phase, applicants attending consular interviews at US embassies, consulates, or Visa Application Centers (VAC / OFC) in cities worldwide (London, New Delhi, Islamabad, Dubai, Manila) must frequently present one or two physical, printed copies. Understanding the differences between these two specifications is essential to avoid administrative processing delays (221g refusal notices).

2. Biometric Geometry: Head Height and Eye Level Calculations

The US Department of State's automated face-detection algorithms scan every photo against an invisible biometric bounding box. Unlike casual selfies or general profile pictures, your face must adhere to strict geometric coordinate boundaries:

The 50% to 69% Head Height Rule

The distance between the bottom of your chin and the top of your head (including hair) must measure between 50% and 69% of the image's vertical dimension. On a standard 600 x 600 pixel digital canvas, this translates to exactly 300 to 414 pixels. If your head is too close to the lens (filling 75% or more), the automated validator rejects the photo for "head too large." If your shoulders occupy too much frame and the head is below 50%, it fails for "head too small."

The Eye Level Coordinate Requirement

Equally critical is the vertical placement of your pupils. The horizontal line connecting the center of both eyes must be positioned between 56% and 69% of the total image height measured from the bottom border (between 336 and 414 pixels on a 600-pixel digital crop). This ensures that biometric facial recognition cameras at US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) ports of entry can match your visa image against electronic passport chips and live airport biometric gates.

3. Lighting, Background, and Composition Rules

Lighting inconsistencies are the leading cause of manual photo rejections by consular officers. To meet ICAO Doc 9303 and State Department standards, your photo must adhere to the following environmental conditions:

  • Plain White or Off-White Backdrop: The background must be completely flat, plain, and free of patterns, wood grain, tile lines, or outdoor scenery. Cream, off-white, or light neutral backdrops are permissible, but pure white (#FFFFFF) provides the highest acceptance probability.
  • Zero Shadows on Face or Background: Shadows cast behind your ears, under your chin, or across your cheeks trigger biometric boundary failures. Use diffused frontal lighting or stand 3 feet in front of a white wall while using a soft ring light or natural daylight from a facing window.
  • Direct Eye Contact with Neutral Expression: You must face the camera directly with both ears visible (or facial profile unobstructed). Tilting your head up, down, or sideways is prohibited. Maintain a relaxed, neutral facial expression with both eyes open and mouth closed. Natural unforced smiles with closed lips are accepted, but wide grins revealing teeth or raised eyebrows cause automated system flags.

4. Clothing, Eyeglasses, and Religious Headwear Policies

Federal regulations strictly dictate what applicants may wear during their photo session:

Official Attire Do's and Don'ts

  • Eyeglasses Strictly Forbidden: Since November 1, 2016, eyeglasses of any kind — prescription glasses, reading glasses, sunglasses, and tinted lenses — are banned. Glare on lenses obscures pupil coordinates in border surveillance databases.
  • No Uniforms or Camouflage: Applicants cannot wear military uniforms, commercial flight uniforms, or clothing resembling official attire, unless it is formal religious clothing worn daily.
  • No Headphones or Wireless Earbuds: AirPods, wireless Bluetooth headsets, and smart glasses must be removed prior to taking the photo.
  • Religious Head Coverings Permitted: Hijabs, turbans, yarmulkes, and religious headwear worn daily for religious purposes are fully allowed. However, the applicant must submit a signed statement confirming religious practice, and the covering must not cast shadows over the face or obscure the hairline, forehead, cheeks, or chin.
  • Medical Devices: Hearing aids or similar devices worn continuously are acceptable.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What are the exact digital pixel dimensions for the DS-160 visa application photo?

The digital photo submitted for the US DS-160 visa application must be square with minimum dimensions of 600 x 600 pixels and maximum dimensions of 1200 x 1200 pixels. The file size must be less than or equal to 240 KB, in standard JPEG (sRGB 24-bit color) format.

What are the physical dimensions for the printed US visa photo?

When attending an in-person US embassy or consulate interview, the physical photo must measure exactly 2 x 2 inches (51 x 51 mm), printed on high-quality glossy or matte photo-quality paper without borders.

Can I wear eyeglasses in a US visa photo?

No. Effective November 1, 2016, eyeglasses are strictly prohibited in US passport and visa photos. The only rare exception is a signed medical statement explaining an urgent medical condition (such as recent eye surgery), and the frames must not obscure the eyes.

What is the head height requirement for a US visa photo?

Your head height (measured from the bottom of the chin to the top of the hair) must occupy between 50% and 69% of the photo's total height (1 inch to 1 3/8 inches on a 2x2 inch print). Additionally, the eye level must sit between 56% and 69% from the bottom of the photo.

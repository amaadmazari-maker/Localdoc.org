1. The Architecture of the Casio fx-5800P Super-FX Plus

Introduced by Casio as a premier natural textbook programmable calculator, the fx-5800P bridges the gap between traditional non-programmable scientific calculators (like the fx-991EX) and bulky graphing calculators. Its signature feature is a high-resolution 4-line dot-matrix LCD display paired with Casio BASIC programming language capabilities.

📟 The 4-Line Dot-Matrix Display Explained

  • Line 1 (Status Flags & Header): Displays active modes (PRGM, D, R, G, S for Shift, A for Alpha, M for Memory, and DEC/HEX in Base-N).
  • Line 2 (Expression & Input Prompt): Displays natural mathematical expressions or user input prompts (e.g. A? 15.5) with a blinking block cursor.
  • Line 3 (Intermediate Step & Pause Indicator): Displays sub-calculations or pauses output with the blinking ◢ symbol until you press EXE.
  • Line 4 (Final Result & Ans): Shows right-aligned numerical output or Disp indicating program execution completion.

3. Ready-to-Use Engineering Program Codes

Here are four essential field programs that you can enter directly into slots P1–P4 on the LocalDoc fx-5800P Calculator:

Program 1: Complete Quadratic Equation Solver (P1: QUAD)

?→A:?→B:?→C:(B²-4AC)→D:(-B+√D)/(2A)→X:(-B-√D)/(2A)→Y:"X1="X◢"X2="Y◢

How it works: Prompts for coefficients $a$, $b$, and $c$, evaluates the discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$, computes both roots, and presents $X_1$ and $X_2$ consecutively on the LCD.

Program 2: Heron's Formula for Irregular Land Parcels (P2: HERON)

?→A:?→B:?→C:(A+B+C)/2→S:√(S(S-A)(S-B)(S-C))→X:"AREA="X◢

How it works: Widely used by surveyors to calculate parcel acreage from three measured boundaries without needing angles.

Program 3: Electrical Power & Voltage Calculator (P3: OHM)

?→I:?→R:I*R→V:V*I→P:"VOLT="V◢"WATT="P◢

How it works: Solves Ohm's Law ($V = IR$) and Joule Heating ($P = VI$) given Current and Resistance.

Program 4: Land Surveying Coordinate Inverse & Bearing (P4: SURVEY-INV)

?→A:?→B:?→C:?→D:Pol(A-C,B-D)→R:Y→T:T<0=>T+360→T:"DIST="R◢"BRG="T◢

How it works: Solves the fundamental traverse inverse between two known control points. Prompts for Station 1 Northing (A) and Easting (B), then Station 2 Northing (C) and Easting (D). Computes horizontal distance $R = \sqrt{(A-C)^2 + (B-D)^2}$ and whole-circle bearing $T$. The conditional branch T<0=>T+360→T normalizes south-west and north-west bearings into standard $0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$ civil engineering azimuth format.

Worked Field Survey Example:
Given STN 1 $(N_1 = 41512.000\text{ m}, E_1 = 48686.000\text{ m})$ and STN 2 $(N_2 = 41412.000\text{ m}, E_2 = 48586.000\text{ m})$:
• Inputs: A = 41512, B = 48686, C = 41412, D = 48586.
• Difference: $\Delta N = 41512 - 41412 = 100.000\text{ m}$, $\Delta E = 48686 - 48586 = 100.000\text{ m}$.
• LCD Output 1: DIST = 141.421 m (pause symbol ◢ blinks; press EXE).
• LCD Output 2: BRG = 45°00'00" (Whole-Circle Bearing North-East).

4. Land Surveying Traversing: Polar vs Rectangular (Pol & Rec)

Field surveyors, geomatics engineers, and civil site teams rely on the Casio fx-5800P to calculate traverse lines, road curves, boundary closures, and staking-out radiation coordinates directly on construction sites.

4.1 Coordinate Inverse vs Forward Radiation

Two primary calculations underpin all land and engineering surveys:

  • Coordinate Inverse (Polar Transformation - Pol): Given coordinates for two control points $(N_1, E_1)$ and $(N_2, E_2)$, determine horizontal distance $r$ and azimuth $\theta$:
    $$\Delta N = N_1 - N_2, \quad \Delta E = E_1 - E_2$$ $$r = \sqrt{(\Delta N)^2 + (\Delta E)^2}, \quad \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{\Delta E}{\Delta N}\right)$$
  • Forward Radiation / Traversing (Rectangular Transformation - Rec): Given an occupied instrument station $(N_0, E_0)$, target distance $D$, and bearing $\theta$, determine the coordinates of the new station:
    $$\Delta N = D \cos(\theta), \quad \Delta E = D \sin(\theta)$$ $$N_1 = N_0 + \Delta N, \quad E_1 = E_0 + \Delta E$$

🧭 Hardware Memory Register Conventions in Casio Calculators

Authentic Casio calculators follow a strict memory assignment convention that simplifies chained field calculations:

  • When evaluating Pol(ΔN, ΔE): Distance $r$ is saved into memory register X, and azimuth angle $\theta$ is saved into memory register Y. Both can be recalled at any time via RCL → X and RCL → Y.
  • When evaluating Rec(Distance, Bearing): Latitude ($\Delta N$) is saved into memory register X, and Departure ($\Delta E$) is saved into memory register Y.
  • Dual-Line LCD Output: On the LocalDoc fx-5800P, Line 3 displays distance $r$ while Line 4 displays $\theta$ simultaneously in decimal degrees and sexagesimal DMS (e.g. 45°00'00").

4.2 Working with Degrees, Minutes, Seconds (DMS - ° ' ")

Surveying bearings and angles are universally recorded in sexagesimal units: degrees ($^\circ$), minutes ($'$), and seconds ($''$).

  • Entering Angles: To enter $124^\circ 45' 30''$, type 124 [° ' "] 45 [° ' "] 30 [° ' "].
  • Toggling DMS on Screen: After computing an angle (e.g. $124.7583^\circ$), press the dedicated ° ' " (DMS) key to toggle between decimal degrees and sexagesimal notation.
  • Whole Circle Bearing (WCB): Azimuth measured clockwise from True or Grid North ($0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$). If your calculation yields a negative bearing (such as $-45^\circ$), adding $360^\circ$ gives the standard azimuth of $315^\circ00'00''$ (North-West).

5. Programmer Base-N Digital Arithmetic

Computer scientists, network administrators, and embedded systems engineers frequently need to work across multiple number systems. In BASE-N mode (press MODE → 2:BASE-N), the fx-5800P functions as a dedicated digital logic workstation:

  • Instant Hardware Switching: Press x² (DEC), log (HEX), ln (BIN), or xʸ (OCT) to convert active values immediately across bases.
  • Bitwise Operators: Calculate network subnet masks, IP ranges, and microcontroller bit flags using AND, OR, XOR, NOT, and NEG.
  • Two's Complement: Negative binary numbers are represented in standard 32-bit two's complement format.

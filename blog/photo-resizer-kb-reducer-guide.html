2. Mathematical Mechanics: 7-Iteration Binary Search Compression

JPEG compression uses Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) quantization matrices parameterized by an empirical quality factor $Q \in [0.05, 0.98]$. The relationship between $Q$ and final file size is highly non-linear and depends on image entropy�facial wrinkles, hair texture, and background noise:

The LocalDoc Binary Search Algorithm

  1. Canvas Cropping: The original image is drawn to an off-screen HTML5 Canvas at the exact requested pixel dimensions (e.g., 413 × 531 px for 35 × 45 mm at 300 DPI).
  2. Bounds Initialization: Lower bound $Q_{\min} = 0.05$; Upper bound $Q_{\max} = 0.98$.
  3. Midpoint Sampling: The midpoint $Q_{\text{mid}} = \frac{Q_{\min} + Q_{\max}}{2}$ is tested using canvas.toBlob('image/jpeg', Q_mid).
  4. Boundary Convergence: If the generated blob size exceeds the target threshold (e.g., 20,480 bytes for a 20 KB limit), $Q_{\max}$ drops to $Q_{\text{mid}}$. If the blob fits under the limit, $Q_{\min}$ increases to $Q_{\text{mid}}$ to maximize optical clarity.
  5. Logarithmic Termination: Because $\log_2(100) \approx 6.64$, the engine converges on the highest possible quality factor within exactly 7 iterations (under 100 milliseconds) with sub-kilobyte accuracy.

4. Honest Technical Limitations: Where Compression Meets Physical Physics

3 Real Limitations to Keep in Mind

  • Severe JPEG Macroblock Noise Below 15 KB on Textured Backdrops: If your photo includes a textured brick wall, foliage, or noisy wallpaper, high-frequency spatial detail consumes available byte budget rapidly. When forcing a textured photo under 15 KB, $8 \times 8$ pixel quantization blocks become visible across facial contours. You must replace the backdrop with a solid studio white or light blue preset before compressing.
  • Tiny Aspect Crops (<100×100 px): If a portal demands sub-10 KB sizes and an applicant crops only a tiny 80 × 80 pixel thumbnail, printing that image at physical 300 DPI will result in severe visual pixelation. Always crop to standard physical millimeter specifications first.
  • Transparent Signatures Cannot Be Saved as JPEG: Government portals frequently require signature uploads under 20 KB. While JPEG achieves higher compression ratios, the JPEG format specification does not support alpha channel transparency. Signatures requiring transparent backgrounds must be exported as PNG, which LocalDoc supports via 8-bit paletted PNG quantization.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert millimeters to pixels for printing–

The standard formula is: $\text{Pixels} = \frac{\text{Millimeters}}{25.4} \times \text{DPI}$. At official consular 300 DPI print quality, a 35mm width equals $\frac{35}{25.4} \times 300 \approx 413$ pixels, and a 45mm height equals $\frac{45}{25.4} \times 300 \approx 531$ pixels. Our tool handles this conversion automatically.

Will compressing to 20KB make my face blurry–

No, because our engine crops the dimensions to the exact pixel standard first before compressing. This ensures that high pixel density is maintained without unnecessary empty canvas data, preserving sharp facial features even under 20KB.

Can I change the photo background to pure white or light blue–

Yes. In the control panel of the Photo Resizer, you can select between Original, White, or Light Blue background presets with a single click.

Is my portrait safe from facial recognition scrapers–

Yes. LocalDoc operates entirely on your physical machine. Unlike commercial converters that store photos on cloud storage buckets, zero bytes leave your computer, guaranteeing complete biometric confidentiality.

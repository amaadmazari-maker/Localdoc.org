Critical Verification Takeaways

1. The Frustrating Banking KYC Rejection

Anyone applying online for a bank account, mortgage, or international visa has encountered this rigid requirement: "Upload a single 1-page document containing both the front and back of your photo ID."

In physical bank branches, clerks solved this using photocopiers: scanning the card face down, flipping it over, and refeeding the paper. For remote smartphone applicants, this requirement creates immediate friction. Taking two photos produces two separate JPEG files. When you attempt to upload them, the onboarding portal provides only a single upload slot.

Desperate applicants often turn to online image-merging websites, uploading unencrypted ID photos to untrusted servers. Identity theft syndicates target these converter logs to harvest national identity numbers and portraits. LocalDoc eliminates this risk by performing geometric alignment and A4 compilation entirely inside your browser's local RAM.

2. Understanding Physical ID-1 Dimensions (ISO/IEC 7810)

A compliant submission must adhere to the physical dimensions defined in ISO/IEC 7810 for ID-1 format cards:

Standard ID-1 Card Physical Geometry:
• Width: 85.60 mm (242.65 PostScript points)
• Height: 53.98 mm (153.02 PostScript points)
• Corner Radius: 3.18 mm (rounded corners)

A4 Standard Sheet Geometry (ISO 216):
• Dimensions: 210.00 × 297.00 mm (595.28 × 841.89 points)
• Placement: Centered horizontally with 20.00 mm vertical margin

When an institution prints your verification sheet, the printed card images must match the physical plastic card. If an online tool stretches your card images to fit page margins, KYC fraud systems will reject the submission as an altered credential.

3. The Client-Side Homography & Alignment Pipeline

Smartphone snapshots of ID cards are rarely captured from a perfectly flat, top-down angle. To produce a clean document, LocalDoc executes a client-side vision pipeline in browser memory:

  1. Corner Detection: Identifies the four plastic corners of the card on the table surface.
  2. Perspective Homography Transformation: Using projective transformation matrices on an OffscreenCanvas, the keystoned shape is flattened into a perfect 85.6 × 53.98 mm rectangle.
  3. Luminosity Balancing: Eliminates glare gradients while keeping microprint patterns and machine-readable zones (MRZ) sharp.
  4. A4 Coordinate Staging: The front card is placed in the upper quadrant, and the back card is centered below it with a standardized 20mm margin.

  1. Capture Sharp Photos: Place your ID card on a dark, flat surface. Take one photo of the front and one of the back under even lighting without flash.

7. Honest Technical Boundaries of Client-Side ID Scanning

Engineering integrity requires noting the physical constraints of smartphone document capture:

  • Specular Flash Glare Destruction: Using your phone's LED camera flash directly above a laminated ID creates white reflective hotspots that erase numbers. Always capture photos under ambient daylight without direct flash.
  • Chipped or Fractured Card Corners: Automated corner detection looks for sharp 90-degree transitions. If an old card has chipped corners, manual pin adjustment is required to map the rectangular homography boundaries.
  • Printer Scaling Warning: LocalDoc compiles PDFs at exact 1:1 physical dimensions (85.6 × 53.98 mm). When printing, always select "Actual Size (100%)" rather than "Fit to Page" in your print dialog to prevent dimensional shrinking.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Why do bank portals reject separate front and back ID photos?

Automated KYC compliance systems and human verification officers require both sides of an ID document on a single printable sheet to verify matching serial numbers, alignment signatures, and machine-readable zones without opening multiple viewer tabs.

Can I scan driver's licenses and residency permits with this tool?

Yes. Any standard plastic card adhering to credit card dimensions (85.60 × 53.98 mm)—including driver's licenses, green cards, national insurance cards, and health cards—can be combined onto a single page using this workflow.

Is my identity card data safe during processing?

Completely safe. LocalDoc processes your card photos 100% inside your device's browser tab RAM. No images, numbers, or biometric data are transmitted over the internet or saved to external servers.

Can I add a security watermark across the combined ID page?

Yes. After generating your combined A4 PDF, you can open our Watermark PDF Tool to stamp text like "FOR VERIFICATION AT ACME BANK ONLY" across the page to prevent fraudulent reuse.

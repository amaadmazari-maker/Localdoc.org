1. The Architecture of NADRA Pak-ID Biometrics & ICAO Standards

Pakistan's NADRA manages one of the world's largest biometric repositories. Whether renewing a Smart National Identity Card (SNIC), applying for an overseas NICOP, or filing a B-Form, submitted portraits are evaluated against ICAO Doc 9303 biometric standards:

  • Interpupillary Distance (IPD): The optical distance between pupil centers must equal or exceed 90 pixels at 300 DPI for neural feature extraction.
  • Vertical Head Allocation (70%–80%): The distance from chin base to crown must occupy between 32 mm and 36 mm of the physical 45 mm frame. Ratios below 65% or above 85% reject automatically.
  • Facial Symmetry: The horizontal eye axis must have zero tilt, and facial yaw (turning side-to-side) must remain under 3 degrees.

2. Background Color Protocols: Pure White vs. Light Blue

Consular and registration guidelines depend on your application channel:

Color Space & Usage Rules

  • Pak-ID App & Web Portal: Requires a Pure White Background (#FFFFFF). The automated segmentation model isolates facial contours; off-white wall textures are flagged as defects.
  • Physical Registration Centres (NRC): Traditionally use Light Blue (#E0F2FE). If preparing physical prints for in-person submission at an NRC or Embassy desk, light blue remains preferred for printed smart cards.
  • LocalDoc Background Leveler: One-click switching between pure optical white (#FFFFFF) and official NADRA light blue (#E0F2FE) directly in browser memory.

5. Honest Technical Limitations: What Automated CNIC Tools Cannot Fix

Professional engineering requires acknowledging the physical boundaries of browser-based processing. Before processing your documents, note these three irreducible constraints:

3 Real Limitations to Keep in Mind

  • Physically Scratched or Chipped Cards: If the protective lamination on your physical card is peeled, or if the engraved 13-digit national identity number (e.g., 42101-XXXXXXX-X) has suffered mechanical abrasion, browser image filters cannot reconstruct missing vector typography. You must request a physical Smart Card duplicate via the Pak-ID portal.
  • Downward Ceiling Fixture Shadows: If your portrait was captured under overhead recessed lighting or a ceiling fan lamp, harsh shadows fall beneath the eye sockets and nose. While our algorithm balances background canvas pixels, it cannot selectively relight facial depressions without triggering biometric contrast anomalies during NADRA automated ingestion. Always illuminate the face with front-facing natural window daylight.
  • Laminated Specular Flash Glare: Taking a photograph of a physical identity card with camera flash enabled produces intense white specular hotspots on the plastic coating. These reflections blind OCR engines and destroy the underlying micro-text security printing. Always photograph ID cards in diffused ambient lighting without flash.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for overseas NICOP online using this photo?

Yes. Photographs generated by LocalDoc's CNIC preset strictly meet Pak-ID online specifications (354×472 px @ 300 DPI, 70%–80% head height, white background) and pass automated quality checks for Pakistani diaspora living in the UK, US, Canada, UAE, and Saudi Arabia.

Is a light blue background required for all CNIC applications?

No. While light blue (#E0F2FE) was mandatory for legacy laminated green ID cards, modern digital SNIC registration centers and the Pak-ID web app explicitly recommend pure white backgrounds. LocalDoc provides one-click switching between White and Light Blue.

What are the rules regarding Hijab, Niqab, and Dupatta?

Headscarves and dupattas are completely permissible for religious observance. However, the forehead, eyebrows, eyes, nose, cheeks, and chin must remain fully exposed. The niqab (face veil) must be temporarily removed for the photograph.

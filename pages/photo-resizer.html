Technical Architecture: Exact KB Binary Search & Canvas Resampling

Most online photo resizers rely on crude one-step raster downsampling that either blurs fine facial features or fails to satisfy strict government portal file-size restrictions (e.g. "must be between 20 KB and 50 KB"). LocalDoc implements a two-stage mathematical processing engine in client JavaScript:

  • Physical Metric to Pixel Conversion (DPI Calculation): When dimensions are specified in millimeters, centimeters, or inches, LocalDoc computes the exact raster matrix using the standard print formulation: Pixels = (Millimeters / 25.4) × Target_DPI. For a standard 35 × 45 mm passport portrait at 300 DPI, the target raster canvas is sized to exactly 413 × 531 pixels.
  • Bicubic Anti-Aliasing Canvas Filtering: High-density camera photos undergo multi-step step-down interpolation on an off-screen HTML5 CanvasRenderingContext2D with imageSmoothingEnabled set to high quality, preventing moiré artifacts and staircasing along hair and facial contours.
  • Binary Search Quantization Matrix (ITU-T T.81 / RFC 2083): Compressing to an exact file size is an inverse optimization problem. LocalDoc executes a logarithmic binary search over JPEG quality factor Q ∈ [0.05, 0.98]. In 5 to 7 rapid iterations (~80ms total), it discovers the highest quality quantization table whose resulting byte stream fits strictly within your target KB threshold.
  • Zero Network Exposure: All pixel manipulations happen in volatile client RAM. No images are posted to remote PHP, Python, or Node.js backend servers, fully eliminating biometric identity theft vectors.

Frequently Asked Questions About Photo Resizing & KB Reduction

How can I compress a photo to exactly under 20 KB or 50 KB–

Under the "Maximum File Size" section, simply select the preset button for "≤ 50 KB" or type "20" into the KB input field. LocalDoc's binary search optimizer will automatically identify the highest quality JPEG compression ratio that stays strictly under your requested byte limit.

Will resizing stretch or distort my face–

No. When you choose standard presets like 35 × 45 mm or 2 × 2 inches, LocalDoc maintains true aspect ratio scaling with centered canvas fitting. It does not squeeze or stretch facial proportions, which would result in immediate biometric rejection by passport authorities.

Can I convert PNG photos into lightweight JPEGs–

Yes. By default, the output format is set to JPG/JPEG because PNG files use lossless compression that cannot be downscaled to 20 KB – 50 KB for complex portraits. The tool automatically flattens transparent backgrounds onto pure white for official compliance.

What DPI should I use for official government applications–

Standard print submissions require 300 DPI (Dots Per Inch) or 600 DPI. For digital portal uploads (such as the US DS-160 or UK Passport Service), pixel dimensions (such as 600 × 600 px) are prioritized over embedded physical DPI tags.

Why did my portal reject my photo even though the dimensions match–

Most automatic rejections are caused by file size being 1 or 2 bytes over the portal's ceiling (e.g. 50.1 KB when the portal demands ≤ 50.0 KB), or incorrect background lighting. LocalDoc's target KB limiter ensures the file is safely under the limit by reserving a small safety margin.

Biometric Guidelines & Image Quality Notice

Low-Resolution Originals: If your starting picture is smaller than 400 × 400 pixels, scaling it up to 600 × 600 pixels will cause visible pixelation. Always capture your original portrait in good lighting with your smartphone's primary rear camera or a digital camera before resizing.

Strict Head-to-Height Ratios: Standard passport photos mandate that the applicant's head (from the bottom of the chin to the top of the hair) must occupy between 70% and 80% of the photo's total vertical height. Crop your photo appropriately before final KB compression.

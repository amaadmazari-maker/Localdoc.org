How to Edit and Sign a PDF Online — Step by Step

  1. Open your PDF document — Drag and drop your target file into the editor workspace above, or tap "Select PDF Document".
  2. Navigate to desired page — Use the page navigation bar to preview and jump directly to the contract page requiring signature or annotations.
  3. Add text, signature, or dates — Select the text tool to type on blank form lines, or open the signature pad to draw a legally compliant signature mark.
  4. Position and resize elements — Drag annotations into place and use the corner handles to adjust visual proportions perfectly.
  5. Export and save — Click "Download Signed PDF" to instantly compile and save your final document directly to your device storage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this online PDF editor legally binding for signatures?

Yes. In most global jurisdictions, including under the US ESIGN Act and European eIDAS regulations, electronic signatures embedded directly into digital agreements are legally binding when there is clear intent by the signatory.

Can I edit existing typed text inside an existing PDF?

Our editor specializes in annotation, form completion, and signature overlays. To replace existing hardcoded underlying text strings, we recommend using our PDF to Word converter, updating the text in DOCX, and converting back to PDF.

Are my sensitive documents uploaded to any remote server?

Never. LocalDoc was engineered from day one with a zero-upload security model. All parsing, canvas rendering, signature embedding, and file compilation occur strictly inside your device's browser memory.

Does this tool work on mobile devices and touchscreens?

Yes. The signature pad and annotation overlay are fully optimized for touch events on iOS Safari, iPadOS, Android Chrome, and Windows touchscreens, allowing natural finger or stylus signing.

Can I sign multi-page PDF documents?

Yes. You can navigate through all pages of your multi-page document, place signatures and text notes across multiple pages, and export the entire consolidated file in a single download.

Technical Architecture: ICAO 9303 Compliance & Canvas 2D Precision

Biometric passport processing requires strict geometric precision. When consular software inspects a submitted digital photo, it executes automated facial recognition checks verifying inter-pupillary distance, head-to-chin vertical millimeter bounds, and background color uniformity. Here is how LocalDoc enforces compliance entirely within client-side JavaScript:

ICAO Doc 9303 Geometric Proportions

International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) standards mandate that the subject's head height (from bottom of chin to top of hair) must occupy between 70% and 80% of total vertical photo height (or 50% to 69% for US 2x2" standards). LocalDoc's interactive overlay renders mathematical SVG viewBox guides dynamically mapped to each preset's exact aspect ratio.

300 DPI Master Rasterization

While the on-screen preview renders at display resolution for smooth 60 FPS touch dragging, final generation constructs an off-screen HTML5 Canvas calibrated at exactly 300 physical dots per inch (e.g., 600 × 600 pixels for US 2x2", 413 × 531 pixels for UK 35x45mm). This ensures zero pixelation when printed on professional dye-sublimation photo paper.

Alpha-Blended Suit Vector Overlay

When formal attire is selected, vectorized SVG male and female business suit templates are composited onto the canvas using Porter-Duff source-over composite operations. The user-adjustable vertical offset slider allows pixel-perfect collar matching regardless of neck length or posture.

2-in-1 ID Card Photocopy Compiler

In addition to passport portraits, LocalDoc includes a dedicated double-sided ID card generator. It renders front and back driver's licenses, national ID cards (such as Pakistan CNIC or EU National Cards) onto a single standardized 300 DPI A4 sheet (2480 × 3508 pixels) at exactly 100% life-size scale for official notarization and legal paperwork.

Common Practical Questions & Technical Troubleshooting

Expert answers to frequent technical requirements and submission guidelines:

How does downloading the 4x6" print sheet save money at local pharmacies?

Retail pharmacies and convenience stores (such as Walgreens, CVS, Boots, or Walmart) often charge $16 to $22 when you ask for "Passport Photos." However, printing a standard 4×6 inch digital photo snapshot at the same kiosk typically costs only $0.35 to $0.40. By generating LocalDoc's 4×6" sheet (which contains 6 perfectly sized 2×2" or 8 35×45mm photos with cut lines), you order a standard 4×6 print and cut the photos out yourself with scissors.

Why does the US DS-160 online visa portal reject my photo upload?

The US Department of State DS-160 online application enforces strict file constraints:
1. Dimensions: Must be square, between 600 × 600 and 1200 × 1200 pixels.
2. File size limit: Must be equal to or less than 240 KB.
3. Color space: Standard sRGB color space.
LocalDoc's single photo export produces a 600 × 600 pixel image optimized below 200 KB, passing all DS-160 automated validator tests.

Can I wear prescription glasses in my passport photo?

No. Under US Department of State, UK HMPO, and Schengen passport rules, eyeglasses are strictly prohibited in passport photos to prevent flash reflection, frame shadow, or obscured iris landmarks. The only exception is documented medical necessity (such as recent eye surgery), which requires a signed physician's statement.

What is the best way to photograph an infant or toddler for a passport?

For babies unable to sit unassisted, lay a clean white or off-white sheet flat on a bed or floor, and lay the child on their back looking upward. Take the photo from directly above at eye level. Ensure both ears or full face symmetry is visible and no hands, toys, or parent fingers appear in the frame. LocalDoc allows you to fine-tune zoom and rotation to achieve the required head proportion.

How does the 2-in-1 ID Card Photocopy mode work for legal documents?

The 2-in-1 ID Card Studio lets you upload the front side and back side of any driver's license, CNIC, or health insurance card. It composites both sides centered onto a single standardized 300 DPI A4 sheet with official document title headers and verification borders, ready for instant printing on any home or office printer without a specialized duplex photocopier.

Are my face photos or identity cards stored on LocalDoc's servers?

No. LocalDoc operates entirely on a zero-upload client-side architecture. Your images are loaded into your browser's private memory heap (RAM) via FileReader and rendered on an HTML5 canvas. No files, metadata, or biometric points are ever transmitted or saved to external servers.

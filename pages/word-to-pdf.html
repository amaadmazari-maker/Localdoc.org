Frequently Asked Questions

Does this tool upload my Word file to a server?

No. LocalDoc is a zero-upload platform. Your Word document is processed entirely within your browser using JavaScript and WebAssembly. No data is transmitted to any server at any point during conversion.

What Word file formats are supported?

The converter supports .docx (Word 2007 and later) files. Legacy .doc formats from Word 97–2003 may have limited support. For best results, save your document as .docx before converting.

Will my fonts and formatting look correct in the PDF?

Yes. The converter preserves headings, paragraphs, bullet lists, bold/italic text, and table structures. Complex custom fonts embedded in the DOCX may render using system font fallbacks in the output PDF.

Is there a file size limit?

There is no hard limit imposed by LocalDoc. However, very large files (over 50MB) may take longer depending on your device's RAM and browser performance. For optimal speed, files under 20MB convert in seconds.

Can I convert Word to PDF on my phone?

Yes. LocalDoc is fully mobile-optimized and works on Android Chrome, Safari on iPhone and iPad, and all modern mobile browsers. No app download is required.

Word to PDF Conversion — Complete Technical Guide

Frequently Asked Technical Questions

Which Word formats does LocalDoc support?
.docx (Office Open XML, Office 2007+) and .doc (Word 97–2003 legacy binary format) are both supported. .dotx and .docm macro-enabled documents are converted without executing macros.
Are hyperlinks preserved in the PDF?
Yes. All internal bookmarks and external hyperlinks in the .docx are preserved as PDF link annotations using the /URI action type in PDF 1.7 specification.
How are embedded images handled?
Embedded images (PNG, JPEG, EMF, WMF) are extracted from the .docx zip container, decoded in WebAssembly, and re-embedded into the PDF as either JPEG or PNG streams depending on the original format.
Does conversion preserve track changes and comments?
Track changes (revisions) are accepted before conversion — the PDF shows the final document state. Comments are optionally included as PDF annotation notes.
